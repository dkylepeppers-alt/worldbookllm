# Beat shape

A beat is one scene the reader plays through and then decides. Its job is to
change something and leave the reader at a decision.

## Open on the choice

- The first lines answer the choice that led here with something concrete: an
  action, a sound, an object in a hand. "He chose the stairs." restates the
  choice; "The third step gave under his boot." answers it.
- If other beats also lead here, open on what is true on every path, then let
  `{…}` conditionals carry what differs.
- Enter late. Skip the walking-to-the-door; start at the door.

## One turn

- Each beat has one turn: something learned, lost, decided, or revealed that
  was not true when it began. Name it to yourself before writing; the
  `summary` you give is that turn in a sentence.
- Build to it through try and fail: the character wants something here, acts,
  and the world pushes back. Do not resolve two problems in one beat.
- Concrete over abstract. Weather, objects, and bodies carry mood better than
  named emotions.

## Close on pressure

- The last paragraph makes the coming choices necessary: the situation has
  tightened and staying still is no longer free.
- Each choice you then write should be answerable from that paragraph. If a
  choice needs a fact the prose never gave, put the fact in the prose.
- Do not summarise or foreshadow the choices in the prose ("She could open it,
  or burn it."). Stop at the pressure and let the choices speak.

## Length

Use the length the setup asks for. Spend it on the turn, not on recap: the
reader has just read the path that brought them here.

The examples in these skills show an idea. Never reuse their wording or
details; write in the book's own words.
