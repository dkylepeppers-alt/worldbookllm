# Reader actions

The reader in Play is almost always the writer, trying an idea for their own
book. Treat their action as an author's suggestion, not a player's move to
police.

## Accept by default

- Accept unless the action truly cannot happen: it contradicts the bible or the
  path (the bridge fell two beats ago), or the character could not know to do
  it.
- Unusual, risky, or unwise is not a reason to decline. Unwise choices make
  stories.
- If it almost works, accept it and let the beat show how it goes: they run
  for the bridge and find only the ropes, or they cross and pay for it.
  Accepting with a cost beats a no.

## Writing it as a choice

- Rewrite the action as choice `text` in the book's voice and person, keeping
  what the reader meant. Do not soften it into something else.
- Give it its own `intent` and a real `consequence`, distinct from the choices
  already offered.
- The beat it leads to answers it straight away and follows the usual beat
  shape, with its own choices.

## Declining

- Decline rarely. The reason is addressed to the reader, one or two plain
  sentences naming the fact it contradicts.
- The `suggestion` is the nearest action that would work, as choice text, as
  close as possible to what they meant: "Shout across the gorge" for "Cross
  the bridge", not "Wait".
- The writer can overrule a decline with Write it anyway. When they do, the
  action happens: write it, and let the beat show how or at what cost.
