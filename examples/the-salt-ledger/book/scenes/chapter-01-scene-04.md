# The Confrontation

## Purpose

Ilse Varga confronts Wren in the cellar. She admits she wondered who would find the truth, and thought it might be Wren. She warns Wren that a ship comes in on tomorrow night's tide, and that Wren should think about what happens if she does not let it. Varga leaves Wren alone with the docket and the harbor bell.

## Continuity Notes

- Varga's step is unhurried, slightly heavier on the left foot — Wren recognizes it before she sees the lamp
- Varga wears her grey coat buttoned to the throat; the heron ring catches the light
- Varga is tired and looks older in lamplight than upstairs
- She does not deny the arrangement; she notes that no clerk in nineteen years has asked
- She tells Wren to put the box back where she found it because the tide comes in under that wall
- The chapter ends on Wren alone in the cellar, the harbor bell counting the turn of the tide
