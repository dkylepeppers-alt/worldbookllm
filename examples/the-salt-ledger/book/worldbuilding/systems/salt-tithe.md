# Salt Tithe

The harbor duty levied on all cargo entering Harrowmere. Every ship that comes into the harbor pays the salt tithe, a duty weighed against its cargo and recorded in the Salt Ledger under the seal of the assessor who cleared it.

## Overview

The salt tithe is the primary source of revenue for the harbor's administration. It is not optional, and it is not secret — every merchant captain expects to pay it. The tithe is assessed by a weigher on the quay, recorded on a docket, signed by the ship's master, and brought up to the tithe desk in the Customs House to be copied into the Salt Ledger and sealed.

## Rules & Limitations

- Every ship pays, every quarter
- The weigher measures the cargo and writes the initial docket on the quay
- The ship's master signs the docket
- A clerk at the tithe desk copies the entry into the Salt Ledger
- The Master Assessor seals the entry with her ring in grey wax
- The money goes into the harbor account
- At year-end, someone reconciles the ledger against the account

The system relies on trust and repetition: clerks copy what is on the docket, the seal confirms the assessor's approval, and the account either balances or it does not. It is designed to catch errors, not fraud — especially not fraud that pays the correct amount on time, every time, for nineteen years.

## History

The salt tithe has been collected for as long as the Customs House has existed — younger than the Tidewardens, but still generations old. The system has been refined into a ritual of paper, wax, and columns that either add up or do not. The _Margery Lane_ anomaly has been hiding in plain sight within this system since the ship's supposed loss.

## Practitioners

- **Weighers** — On the quay, measure cargo and write dockets
- **Clerks** — At the tithe desk, copy entries into the Salt Ledger
- **Master Assessor** — Reviews and seals entries with her ring
- **Ships' masters** — Sign the dockets to confirm their cargo

## Impact on Society

The salt tithe is the financial lifeblood of the harbor. It is also the perfect cover for a covert operation: a payment that looks legitimate because it is legitimate, recorded in the official ledger, balancing the official account, but attached to a ship that does not exist. The system's very reliability — its predictability, its trust in routine — is what has allowed the _Margery Lane_ fraud to continue undetected for nineteen years.
