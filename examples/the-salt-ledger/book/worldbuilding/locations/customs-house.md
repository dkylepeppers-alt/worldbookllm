# Customs House

The Customs House of Harrowmere keeps its books the way the sea keeps its dead: patiently, and with no intention of giving anything back. It sits on the harbor, a building of ledgers, dockets, and sealed records.

## Description

A working government building with multiple floors and a cellar level that floods at high tide. The ledger room is on the top floor, under the slates, where in the evenings the only sound is the harbor bell counting the tide. The cellars smell of brine and old paper; boxes of dockets go back further than the ledgers, some swollen and split where water has come in under the foundations.

## History

Younger than the Tidewardens, the Customs House was established to formalize the collection of harbor duties. It has processed the salt tithe for generations, recording every ship's cargo in the Salt Ledger under the seal of the assessor who cleared it.

## Culture & Customs

- The year-end reconciliation is dreaded work that no one wants; it falls to the most junior clerk
- Every tithe begins with a docket: a slip written on the quay by the weigher, signed by the ship's master, brought up to the tithe desk to be copied and sealed
- Dockets are filed in boxes by year and kept in the cellars, rarely looked at again
- Clerks work at tithe desks, copying entries into the Salt Ledger
- Master Assessors review ledgers and place their seal in grey wax — a heron standing on one leg

## Notable Features

- **Ledger room:** Top floor, under the slates. Quiet in the evenings. Shelves hold volumes of the Salt Ledger going back years.
- **Tithe desk:** Where clerks copy dockets into the ledger. Whichever clerk is on duty that quarter writes the entry.
- **Cellars:** Below ground, where docket boxes are stored by year. Smell of brine and old paper. Some boxes have swollen and split from tidal flooding.
- **Spare nibs drawer:** In Wren's desk downstairs — she keeps Varga's first correction note there.

## Current State

Functioning and outwardly orderly. The harbor account balances, as it always does. But the records contain a nineteen-year anomaly: the _Margery Lane_ pays its tithe every quarter, though the ship sank nineteen years ago. Every entry carries the heron seal of Master Assessor Ilse Varga.
