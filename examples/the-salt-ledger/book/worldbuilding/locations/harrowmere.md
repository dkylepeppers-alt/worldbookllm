# Harrowmere

A tidal port town built around a harbor that keeps its dead patiently and with no intention of giving anything back. The sea defines life here: ships come in, ships go out, and some do not come back.

## Description

A working harbor town with a harbor wall, a fish market, and quays that stretch along the water. The north quay runs past the fish market to berth nine, where the Tidewardens keep their boats. The harbor bell counts the tide from somewhere above the water, and the sound carries through the empty streets at night. High tides come in under the foundations of older buildings — the Customs House cellars smell of brine and old paper, and some boxes of records have swollen and split.

## History

The town has been here long enough that the Tidewardens — the harbor pilots — are older than the Customs House. The harbor has claimed ships before. The most famous is the _Margery Lane_, lost on the Teeth nineteen years ago with eleven men aboard and a hold full of copper. There is a plaque about it on the harbor wall.

## Culture & Customs

- The salt tithe is a duty every ship pays on its cargo, weighed and recorded
- Harbor pilots are essential — nobody brings a ship into Harrowmere without a Tideward at the wheel
- The year-end reconciliation is dreaded work that falls to the most junior clerk
- Fog can sit on the harbor for a week at a time

## Notable Features

- **The Customs House:** Government building on the harbor, with a ledger room under the slates and cellars that flood at high tide
- **The harbor wall:** Bears a plaque commemorating the _Margery Lane_
- **The fish market:** On the north quay, past which berth nine lies
- **Berth nine:** At the far end of the north quay, where the Tidewardens keep their boats. Nobody, as far as Wren knows, has ever brought a ship in to berth nine
- **The Teeth:** A treacherous reef or passage at the harbor mouth that has claimed ships for as long as the town has stood

## Current State

At the time of the story, Harrowmere is a functioning port going about its business. But beneath the surface, a covert arrangement has been running for nineteen years: a ship that does not exist pays its taxes on time, and someone collects.
