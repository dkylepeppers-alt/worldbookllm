# Ilse Varga

Master Assessor at the Customs House of Harrowmere. Has cleared more cargo than anyone in the building. Never raises her voice — she has a way of reading a column of figures aloud, slowly, that makes the clerk who wrote it wish they had chosen another trade. Wears a grey coat buttoned to the throat and a heron ring on her right hand.

## Appearance

- Grey coat buttoned to the throat, always
- Heron ring on her right hand — the hand she uses to point out mistakes
- Looks tired and older in lamplight than she does upstairs
- Unhurried step, slightly heavier on the left foot

## Personality & Traits

- Controlled and deliberate — never surprised, never hurried
- Intellectually sharp — knows every clerk's habits
- Ruthlessly pragmatic — has maintained a secret for nineteen years
- Capable of mentorship — recognized Wren's potential early
- Morally ambiguous — not a villain who cackles, but someone who made a choice long ago and has stuck to it

## Backstory

Has been at the Customs House long enough to have placed the first false _Margery Lane_ entry nineteen years ago. Knows every clerk who has sat at the tithe desk since. Has a system for keeping the secret: the heron seal, the dockets, the payments that balance. She once told Wren, "You see what others skim. Don't stop." Whether that was genuine mentorship or a way of testing whether Wren would eventually find the truth is unclear.

## Motivations & Goals

- **External want:** To keep the _Margery Lane_ arrangement running smoothly
- **Internal need:** To believe that the balance — the harbor account, the peace, the unasked question — is worth the lie
- **Conflict:** She genuinely admires Wren and seems almost relieved that someone has finally asked, yet she warns Wren to let it go

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Quiet, measured, authoritative. Speaks in complete thoughts with pauses that land. Never shouts — the silence does the work.

> "You're never behind."

> "I wondered who it would be. I thought, once, that it might be you."

> "Nineteen years, Wren. Do you know how many clerks have sat at that desk in nineteen years? Every one of them copied that line. Not one of them asked."

> "There is a ship coming in on tomorrow night's tide... You may think about what happens if you don't."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** The respected mentor who has hidden a secret for nineteen years
- **Key turning points:** Wren discovers the fraud; Varga offers a veiled warning rather than a denial
- **Ending state:** TBD — will she protect Wren, silence her, or let the system collapse?
