## Story Kernel

In the tidal port of Harrowmere, nineteen-year-old junior customs clerk Wren Adair has always believed that columns either add up or they do not, and that the reason is written down somewhere if you are willing to look. During a thankless year-end reconciliation in the Customs House cellars, she discovers that the brigantine *Margery Lane* — lost with all hands on the Teeth nineteen years ago, the same ship her father showed her on a harbor plaque as a child — has paid its salt tithe every quarter since, under the heron seal of her mentor, Master Assessor Ilse Varga. Wren wants to understand why a dead ship still pays taxes; what stands in her way is Varga herself, controlled and deliberate, who seems almost relieved that someone has finally asked yet warns her to let it go, and a system that has swallowed nineteen years of clerks who copied the line without question. Tomorrow night the ship comes in again. The tone is quiet, methodical, and increasingly claustrophobic — the dread of a discovery that implicates the only person who ever told you your careful attention mattered.
