## Chapter Text
The flame took the corner first. The cheap grey paper curled inward, the weigher's careful capitals blackening, the looping _Cask_ shrinking to a thread of ash. The pencilled message went last — _Tell O. the lamp is lit_ — the graphite flaring blue-white for an instant before there was nothing but char in the lamp's dish and the smell of burned paper mixing with the cellar's brine.

Wren watched it finish. Then she tipped the lamp's dish into the water that seeped along the wall, and the last of the docket hissed away.

She climbed the stairs with the lamp in one hand and her copied sheet in the other. The corridor was empty; the watchman's bell had rung somewhere above while she was below, and the silence that followed seemed to wait for her to fill it. She did not. She walked to her desk on the clerks' floor and sat in the dark with the sheet unfolded before her, the dates and amounts and names — her own among them — pale in the light from the corridor sconce.

She had told herself it was necessary. The docket was a single point of failure, a piece of paper that could be found in her coat, that could send her to the cells or worse, that Varga had seen her holding. Without it, she was only a girl with a copied sheet and a suspicion. She was safe, the way nineteen years of clerks had been safe, the way she had been safe two years ago when she copied the line without asking and thought nothing of it.

But the copied sheet was still in her hand. The names were still in her handwriting. And somewhere in the ledger room above, her slanted sevens and crossed double-l's sat in the autumn quarter of two years past, part of the same lie she had just fed to the flame.

She sat for a long time, listening to the harbor bell count the tide. The sheet was warm from her pocket, as if the fire had travelled there. Tomorrow night the ship would come in, or something would, and the ledger would balance, and she would be — what? Another clerk who had seen and done nothing? Or something else, something she did not yet have a name for?

The lamp on her desk was cold. She had not lit it. She sat in the dark with the copied sheet and the knowledge that she had chosen once, and would have to choose again.
