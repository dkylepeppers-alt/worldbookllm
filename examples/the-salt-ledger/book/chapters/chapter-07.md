## Chapter Text
Wren gathered the copied sheets and the docket together, her hands steady now that the decision was made. The wall behind the ledger room's east shelf had a gap where the wainscoting met the floor, a cavity left by old heating pipes that had been removed before her time. She had noticed it months ago, dropping a pen nib and finding the space wide enough to swallow her hand. She had never put anything there until now.

She rolled the sheets tight, the docket nested inside like a seed, and slid the cylinder into the gap. It went easily, silent against brick and plaster dust. She pushed it deep, until her fingers grazed the back of the cavity and the roll was lost in darkness. Then she straightened the wainscoting with her palm, brushing away the pale dust that had marked her fingers. The gap looked like any other gap in an old building. The shelf above it held the same volumes, the same order, the same pretense that nothing had been disturbed.

She capped her pen and put it in her pocket. The open ledger she closed and returned to its place, the autumn quarter volume aligned with its neighbors by the worn leather at their spines. The desk was bare. Her lamp was out. She stood in the dark for three breaths, listening to the harbor bell, and then she slid the bolt back and opened the door.

The corridor was empty. The watchman's rounds had passed, or had not yet reached this wing; she did not know which, and it did not matter now. She walked with unhurried steps, the steps of a clerk who had stayed late to finish a reconciliation and was now going home to a cold supper and a colder bed. Her hands were empty. Her coat was flat against her sides. If the watchman turned his lamp her way, he would see a junior clerk with ink on her fingers and nothing else.

The harbor door opened onto the quay and the fog. It had come in while she worked, a grey tide thicker than the one that moved beneath the Customs House cellars. The lamps of the lower harbor were only smears of yellow, and the masts of the moored ships had lost their tops in the dark. Wren pulled her collar up and walked into it, her boots finding the cobbles by memory.

She did not go toward her lodging. Not yet. The night was whole before her, and somewhere in it the truth she had hidden was waiting to be claimed. The fog swallowed her footsteps. The harbor bell rang once, marking some turn of the tide she had not kept track of. She walked with empty hands and a full mind, the heron seal still visible when she blinked, and the ship that would come in tomorrow night moving toward Harrowmere through water she could not see.
